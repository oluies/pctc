Closed timelike curves, numerically

Three quantum models of a closed timelike curve, evaluated in your browser. The same Scala that runs the command-line program, compiled to JavaScript with Scala.js — no server does any of this arithmetic.

Nothing here sends information backward in time. This is a numerical model of the framework in arXiv:2509.08965, built on the post-selected curves of arXiv:1005.2219. There is no closed timelike curve: there is a linear map, a projection onto a maximally entangled state, and a division by the probability of that projection. The physical claim the paper makes is conditional — granted the existence of a noisy post-selected curve, these are the information-theoretic limits — and this computes only the right-hand side.
Higher is slower and more accurate; steps=9, rounds=30 reaches the bisection floor at about 1e-9.

(a) The grandfather paradox

The chronology-violating qubit is rotated by Ry(θ) inside an otherwise empty loop. The loop operator is Tr[Ry(θ)] = 2 cos(θ/2), so the post-selection probability is cos^2(θ/2), and at θ = π the branch has probability exactly zero: the paradox is resolved by never happening. Both facts are proved in Lean.

(b) Post-selected teleportation

A message is Bell-measured against the future end of an entangled pair. That outcome has probability 1/4, and conditioned on it the past end carries the message exactly. Inside the loop that 1/4 is the denominator, which the loop divides out.

Solved by sweeping a Bloch parametrisation and bisecting lambda, not with a semidefinite solver, so every value is an upper bound approached from above. (d) Three models on the same circuits

Deutsch tau = Tr_CR[U (rho (x) tau) U*] always satisfiable, sometimes not uniquely P-CTC rho -> C rho C* / Tr[C rho C*] forbids a paradox outright T-CTC rho -> z[ C rho C* + Tr_CV(U (rho (x) 1) U*) ] never forbids anything, pays in mixedness

Circuits and published solutions from arXiv:1401.4933 (Allen), which works all three models on each. Deutsch's condition is solved as a kernel, never by iteration: for the grandfather paradox the map has period two and an iteration never settles.

(e) What is proved in Lean

The tables above are numerics: they agree with the closed forms and with two independent semidefinite solvers, which is evidence but not proof. The identities they rest on are proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib, and the proofs, together with every Mathlib declaration they depend on, are re-checked by kolla, an independently written Lean kernel. Each rests on Lean's three standard axioms and nothing else.
