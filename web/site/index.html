Closed timelike curves, numerically

Three quantum models of a closed timelike curve, evaluated in your browser. The same Scala that runs the command-line program, compiled to JavaScript with Scala.js — no server does any of this arithmetic. Every symbol is defined, with a reference, under Notation and terms.

Nothing here sends information backward in time. This is a numerical model of the framework in arXiv:2509.08965, built on the post-selected curves of arXiv:1005.2219. There is no closed timelike curve: there is a linear map, a projection onto a maximally entangled state, and a division by the probability of that projection. The physical claim the paper makes is conditional — granted the existence of a noisy post-selected curve, these are the information-theoretic limits — and this computes only the right-hand side.
Higher is slower and more accurate; steps=9, rounds=30 reaches the bisection floor at about 1e-9.

(a) The grandfather paradox

The chronology-violating qubit is rotated by Ry(θ) inside an otherwise empty loop. The loop operator is Tr[Ry(θ)] = 2 cos(θ/2), so the post-selection probability is cos^2(θ/2), and at θ = π the branch has probability exactly zero: the paradox is resolved by never happening. Both facts are proved in Lean.

(b) Post-selected teleportation

A message is Bell-measured against the future end of an entangled pair. That outcome has probability 1/4, and conditioned on it the past end carries the message exactly. Inside the loop that 1/4 is the denominator, which the loop divides out.

(f) Side note: why cat-qubit designs leave out bit-flip protection

Not part of the paper above. Alice & Bob estimate that RSA-2048 can be factored with about 23 000 cat qubits in 3.14 days (IACR ePrint 2026/2665), assuming a phase-flip probability of 5e-4 per cycle and no bit flips. Without bit flips, a repetition code against phase flips alone is enough. This table shows why, on the same density-matrix layer as (a) to (d): a code on n qubits, noise once on every qubit, then a majority vote. Against phase flips it fails only when more than half the qubits flip, so longer codes fail far less often. Against bit flips it is useless: a single flip turns logical |+⟩ into logical |−⟩, the code's checks cannot see it, and longer codes fail more often. The majority-vote facts are proved in Lean.

This is the simplest noise model, a single round with perfect measurement. The paper's figures come from noise during every step of repeated error correction, which this does not reproduce, and the difference is large. The paper fits its repetition code's logical error rate, per logical qubit per n cycles, as 0.12 n (23 p)^(0.99 ⌊(n + 1)/2⌋) (its Eq. (C46), fitted to simulations above about 1e-8 and extrapolated below), and uses n = 15. At p = 5e-4:

The gap widens with the code, from 69 times at n = 3 to about thirty million at n = 15: a single round says the code is far better than it is. 7.87e-16 is the value the paper quotes for its n = 15 configuration (Table XXIV).

And the premise is a bet on hardware. The headline configuration needs a bit-flip time of about a hundred years, a bit-flip probability of about 1.6e-16 per qubit per cycle; the measured cat qubits the paper cites reach between about a millisecond and about 50 seconds, single qubits only, which it calls "more than eight orders of magnitude" short (its Appendix C9). Adding a small outer code against bit flips relaxes the requirement to about 1.3 hours at roughly twice the qubits, the 45 000 figure.

A cheaper decoder gives something up. On nine qubits, the majority of three majorities of three synthesises to 16 generic gates where the exact majority needs 37, but two flips in each of two groups defeat it: it corrects only three flips where the exact majority corrects four, and at p = 5e-4 it fails about 430 times as often, 1.7e-12 against 3.9e-15. Both decoders are built in Chisel in brokkr, and the correction limits are proved in Lean.

Notation and terms

The page writes its formulas in plain text, as the command-line program prints them: (x) is the tensor product ⊗, * the conjugate transpose †, ^2 a square, lambda the scalar λ, and I and 1 the identity matrix. A reference given only as a section, equation or exercise number is to Nielsen & Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information (Cambridge, 2010), cited below as N&C.

Ry(θ)
The single-qubit gate that rotates the Bloch vector by the angle θ about the y axis: Ry(θ) = exp(-iθY/2), the matrix [[cos θ/2, -sin θ/2], [sin θ/2, cos θ/2]], with Y the Pauli matrix. Its trace is 2 cos(θ/2). At θ = π it takes |0⟩ to |1⟩ and back, up to a sign: a bit flip, which is why (a) uses it as the qubit that would undo its own past. N&C §4.2, Eq. (4.5).
Tr, Tr_X
The trace, the sum of a matrix's diagonal entries. Tr_X is the partial trace over subsystem X, which discards X and leaves the state of the rest; Tr_CTC, Tr_CR and Tr_CV discard the curve's system, the chronology-respecting one and the chronology-violating one. N&C §2.4.3.
U, C = Tr_CTC(U), d
U is the unitary acting on the ordinary system together with the system on the curve. Tracing out the curve's system leaves the loop operator C, which acts on the ordinary system alone; d is the dimension of the curve's system. Lloyd et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 106, 040403 (2011), arXiv:1005.2219.
rho, tau
Density matrices: rho the state entering the circuit, tau the state on the curve in Deutsch's model. N&C §2.4.
post-selection, P(post-select)
Keeping only the runs in which a measurement gave a chosen outcome, and renormalising by that outcome's probability. A P-CTC is a curve built this way; P(post-select) is the probability of the kept outcome, the denominator the loop divides by. S. Aaronson, Proc. R. Soc. A 461, 3473 (2005), arXiv:quant-ph/0412187; Lloyd et al., above.
Bell measurement, Phi+, teleportation
A measurement of two qubits in the basis of the four maximally entangled Bell states, of which Phi+ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 is one. Teleportation sends a qubit using a shared Bell pair, a Bell measurement and a correction chosen by its outcome; post-selecting the Phi+ outcome makes the correction unnecessary. N&C §1.3.6 and §1.3.7; Bennett et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 70, 1895 (1993).
fidelity, purity
The fidelity reported is the overlap Tr[ψ rho] with a pure target state ψ, 1 exactly when the two agree; it is the square of the fidelity as N&C define it. The purity is Tr[rho^2]: 1 for a pure state and 1/d for the maximally mixed state of a d-dimensional system. N&C §9.2.2; Exercise 2.71.
N, Phi, π, N(Phi)
N is the noisy channel under study, the curve's noise, from A to B. Phi is the maximally entangled state of A and a copy A′. In Eqs. (9) and (10), π is the maximally mixed state I/d_A on A′, with d_A the dimension of A; next to θ, as in θ = π and θ / π, it is the number π ≈ 3.14159. N(Phi), the channel applied to one half of Phi, is the Choi state, which determines the channel completely. M.-D. Choi, Linear Algebra Appl. 10, 285 (1975); π for the maximally mixed state follows M. M. Wilde, Quantum Information Theory (Cambridge, 2017), arXiv:1106.1445.
sigma_B, tau_B, <=
A state and a unit-trace Hermitian operator on the channel's output B, each optimised over in Eqs. (9) and (10). Between operators, A <= B is the positive semidefinite order: B - A has no negative eigenvalue. J. Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (Cambridge, 2018).
I_max, I_doe, C_retro
The max-information and Doeblin information of the channel, Eqs. (9) and (10), and their sum, Eq. (11), reported here as the retrocausal capacity of the noisy curve. Strictly, the paper's capacity uses the regularised Doeblin information; that equals I_doe for the depolarizing and erasure channels, and for amplitude damping I_doe is a lower bound on it. I_max is a max-relative entropy, minimised over sigma_B. Ji, Lloyd & Wilde, arXiv:2509.08965; N. Datta, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 55, 2816 (2009), for the max-relative entropy.
C_ordinary, beats it
The channel's ordinary classical capacity, with no curve, as Holevo, Schumacher and Westmoreland characterise it. "beats it" says whether C_retro is larger. A. S. Holevo, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 44, 269 (1998); B. Schumacher and M. D. Westmoreland, Phys. Rev. A 56, 131 (1997).
depolarizing, erasure, amplitude damping; p, g
The three noisy channels swept in (c). The depolarizing channel replaces the qubit with the maximally mixed state with probability p; the erasure channel replaces it with an orthogonal "erased" flag with probability p; amplitude damping relaxes |1⟩ to |0⟩ with probability g (γ), as a decaying atom does. N&C §8.3.4 and §8.3.5; Bennett, DiVincenzo & Smolin, Phys. Rev. Lett. 78, 3217 (1997), for erasure.
Bloch parametrisation
Writing a qubit state as (I + xX + yY + zZ)/2, with X, Y and Z the Pauli matrices and x^2 + y^2 + z^2 <= 1, so a search over states becomes a search over a ball. N&C §1.2; Exercise 2.72.
cat qubit
A qubit stored in two coherent states of a microwave resonator, kept apart so that bit flips are exponentially suppressed in the number of photons and almost only phase flips remain. M. Mirrahimi et al., New J. Phys. 16, 045014 (2014), arXiv:1312.2017; J. Guillaud and M. Mirrahimi, Phys. Rev. X 9, 041053 (2019), arXiv:1904.09474.
bit flip, phase flip, dephasing
A bit flip is the error X, which swaps |0⟩ and |1⟩; a phase flip is Z, which swaps |+⟩ and |−⟩. Dephasing applies Z with probability p, rho -> (1 - p) rho + p Z rho Z, and the bit-flip channel does the same with X. N&C §8.3.3.
repetition code, n, majority vote
The phase-flip repetition code on n qubits, n odd, stores logical |0⟩ as |+⟩^n and logical |1⟩ as |−⟩^n, and is read by measuring every qubit in the |±⟩ basis and taking the majority. With each qubit flipped independently with probability p it fails when more than n/2 flip, the binomial tail. N&C §10.1.1 and §10.1.2.
Deutsch, P-CTC, T-CTC
Three models of a quantum closed timelike curve. Deutsch's asks that the state on the curve be a fixed point of the evolution; P-CTCs post-select, as above; Allen's T-CTCs keep the post-selected term and add the ordinary channel to it. In the T-CTC line of (d), 1 is the identity on the curve's system, deliberately not divided by its dimension (Allen, Eq. (33)), and z is the constant that rescales the sum to unit trace. "Deutsch solutions" counts the free parameters of the fixed points, "unique" meaning there is just one; "T coherent" is the share of the T-CTC output that comes from the post-selected term. D. Deutsch, Phys. Rev. D 44, 3197 (1991); Lloyd et al., above; J.-M. A. Allen, Phys. Rev. A 90, 042107 (2014), arXiv:1401.4933.
